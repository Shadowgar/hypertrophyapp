# M4-R Batch 06 — RIR, muscular failure, fatigue and progression: targeted critical appraisal

**Date:** 2026-10-16 (literature through this date). **Status:** research-only, provisional. Continues [Batch 05](batch-05-volume-frequency-low-time-appraisal.md) and the [research protocol](protocol.md). This is **not** a preregistered systematic review, an independent RoB 2/AMSTAR 2/GRADE determination, an implemented workout engine, or permission for numeric prescriptions.

## Research questions and screening provenance

- **Q6-A:** Does training to *momentary concentric failure* uniquely maximize muscle growth versus stopping with an estimated 1–3 repetitions in reserve (RIR)? Are the results different for compound versus isolated movements?
- **Q6-B:** How much do measurement, effort-reporting error and fatigue limit an automatic RIR target?
- **Q6-C:** Can repetitions or load progression both sustain adaptations? Does evidence require increasing the load every calendar week?
- **Q6-D:** Can short-term fatigue or a preplanned deload be used to predict a fixed optimal recovery rule?

## Evidence: chronic muscle-size comparisons

### B06-E1 — 2023 systematic review/meta-analysis, full text read

Refalo et al., [PMID 36334240](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36334240/), [PMC9935748](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9935748/), DOI `10.1007/s40279-022-01784-y`. **15 trials**. Critical distinction: a broad mixture of definitions of “set failure” marginally favored failure (SMD 0.19; 95% CI 0.00–0.37), but the narrower **momentary muscular failure** subgroup did not show a statistically significant advantage (SMD 0.12; CI −0.13 to 0.37). Sensitivity of the mixed-definition pooled result depended on assumptions. Trained status and comparator protocols varied; most studies did not precisely report *real* RIR. Original reviewers used **TESTEX**, not outcome-specific RoB 2; they noted absent allocation concealment and incomplete adherence/withdrawal reporting in several trials. The review's suggested practical volume bands **do not qualify as our app's validated dose defaults**.

**Disposition:** A binary failure/non-failure indicator hides different interventions. “Not significant” does **not** prove equivalence or a personal RIR optimum; pooled averages cannot tell the generator to force every set to failure.

### B06-E2 — 2024 estimated-RIR meta-regression, abstract checked (not full-text appraised here)

Robinson et al., [PMID 38970765](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38970765/), DOI `10.1007/s40279-024-02069-2`. Retrospective **RIR estimates from descriptions**, multilevel exploratory regressions adjusted for selected study features. Nearer failure associated with greater average hypertrophy change; strength association less clear. Models had modest overall fit. Retrospectively inferred RIR is *not* a set-level measured exposure and meta-regression is not a direct experiment on precise thresholds. May share trials with B06-E1 and other reviews.

**Disposition:** Direction-of-association hypothesis, not an exact 0/1/2/3 RIR dose-response engine or an instruction to maximize failure exposure.

### B06-E3 — 2023 trained-adult low- versus high-RIR trial, full text read

Ruple et al., [PMID 37144554](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37144554/), [PMC10161210](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10161210/), DOI `10.14814/phy2.15679`. **19 experienced adults** (11 male, 8 female), five training weeks plus reduced-load week; two group targets roughly **0–1** versus **4–6 RIR** on key compound lifts, with other movements performed at similar conservative effort. No clearly differentiated 1RM gains or vastus lateralis regional muscle cross-sectional-area changes, although motor-unit findings differed. **Critical limits:** small group sizes 10/9, short time course, **unsupervised training**, self-reported completion/RIR, inconsistent directly measured hypertrophy, possible diet confounding, and sample size too small to exclude important contrasts.

**Disposition:** Farther-from-failure training can plausibly produce strength gains under this context; no evidence here to mandate failure or to declare 4–6 RIR universally growth-equivalent to 0–1.

### B06-E4 — 2024 within-person leg study, abstract/publisher summary reviewed

Refalo et al., [PMID 38393985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38393985/), DOI `10.1080/02640414.2024.2321021`. **18 trained men/women**, two sessions weekly for eight weeks; randomized leg press/knee-extension limbs to momentary failure or perceived **2/1 RIR**. Average quadriceps ultrasound change about **0.181 cm** (failure) and **0.182 cm** (RIR) with wide posterior intervals; measured lifting-velocity/repetition loss was greater in failure. Site-specific effects varied in direction. No independent full manuscript/figure bias appraisal performed.

**Disposition:** Supports an exercise-specific non-failure option, not statistical equivalence, an injury-risk coefficient or a universal multi-muscle protocol.

### B06-E5 — 2025 single-set nine-exercise RCT, abstract/publisher summary reviewed

Hermann et al., [PMID 40249908](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40249908/), DOI `10.1249/MSS.0000000000003728`. **42 trained adults**, parallel randomization, nine exercises at **one set each**, twice/week for eight weeks, comparing failure to **2 RIR**. Both conditions improved; some imaging-based growth outcomes modestly favored failure while strength/endurance were broadly similar. Bench-press RIR estimates were more accurate than squat estimates and accuracy changed with practice. Limited volume and short intervention bound interpretation; a *single-set* effect cannot be applied to every set of a high-volume workout. Full article not independently bias-appraised here.

**Disposition:** Conflicting nuance must remain visible: sometimes failure might confer a small local benefit under a constrained single-set protocol. It still does **not** validate always-failure training.

### B06-E6 — 2025 four-arm trained-men trial, published full PDF inspected

Robinson et al., *International Journal of Strength and Conditioning*, [DOI 10.47206/ijsc.v5i1.393](https://doi.org/10.47206/ijsc.v5i1.393), [university repository published PDF](https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/items/baaed4c4-e6eb-4d0d-bec8-bb6fd7e30dca), published **2025-08-29**. **38 recruited men**, four intended groups: 4–6 RIR, 1–3 RIR, last-set failure, all-set failure, training squat/bench 3 times weekly for eight weeks. Crucial *protocol feasibility and evidence validity*: six participants withdrew for training-related discomfort/injury (three each in the latter two groups); **all-set failure ended early with only n=3**, and the last-set-failure lower-body condition was **modified and removed from relevant outcome comparisons**. COVID interrupted recruitment. Multiple sonographers and no calibrated interrater imaging reliability widened uncertainty; some authors disclosed coaching roles.

Reported bench strength gain roughly 9 kg in each non-failure condition versus about 5 kg last-set failure and under 1 kg all-set failure, **but** altered protocols, tiny groups and dropouts bar any conclusive causal hierarchy. **Hypertrophy contrasts remained inconclusive** and objective/subjective *longitudinal* fatigue indices were not clearly different among analyzed conditions. Distinguish these chronic fatigue outcomes from the acute consequences of any given session.

**Disposition:** Strong *feasibility flag*, **not** a validated clinical injury-probability estimate or proof failure always harms growth/strength. Never hide an unsustainable protocol or extrapolate small/modified groups into personal prescriptions.

### B06-E7 — two newly identified 2026 controlled studies, differing muscles and full-text access

- **Elbow flexors:** Vasconcelos et al., [PMID 42784046](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42784046/), [PMC13609929](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13609929/), DOI `10.3390/muscles5030061`; published **2026-08-30**. Full text examined. **23 recruited, 19 analyzed trained adults**, within-person one-arm preacher-curl protocol, twice/week, eight weeks, each arm randomized to failure versus directly coached **1–3 RIR**. Both grew; ultrasound between-protocol mean difference **0.032 cm (95% HDI −0.044 to 0.113)**. Within-person design and supervised familiarization aid relevance; limited ability to detect smaller differences (authors' sensitivity discussion), one movement/site, short duration, uncertain generalization to many high-fatigue compound sets. Non-significant or near-zero difference not blanket equivalence.
- **Knee extensors:** Vasconcelos et al., [PMID 42617172](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42617172/), DOI `10.1519/JSC.0000000000005494`; issue date **2026-10-01**. **19 trained adults**, randomized unilateral leg extensions, eight weeks, failure versus RIR (reported **1–3 RIR**); abstract reported knee extensor ultrasound between-protocol difference **−0.019 cm (interval −0.094 to 0.054)** and no clearly distinguished 1RM result. **Abstract-only** in this appraisal; exact rest, adherence, imaging and full-text allocation/blinding not independently checked.

**UPDATED shared-cohort verification (Batch 07, 2026-10-16):** The arm and leg reports **must be treated as a shared 19-person intervention cohort, not independent replications**. In addition to the identical 11-male/8-female breakdown and ~22.3-year/~69.9-kg cohort averages, a third author-group article [Vasconcelos et al., *Applied Sciences* 2026, DOI 10.3390/app16178724](https://doi.org/10.3390/app16178724) **explicitly describes the same 19 adults simultaneously training unilateral preacher curls and leg extensions for eight weeks**, with the opposite limbs assigned to failure versus RIR. This connects the arm and leg protocols in one source and strengthens the prior probabilistic matching. No identifiable individual participant records or cross-paper IDs were obtained or needed. Treat those three reports (two muscle imaging outcomes plus limb-level bioelectrical outcomes) as **one primary trial cohort with multiple reports**, never 19+19+19 separate people. The bioelectrical outcomes do not demonstrate muscle growth.

**Disposition:** Similar results for two muscle actions in a **single cohort** support conditional movement-specific flexibility, not independent replication, a calibrated universal RIR curve, or equivalence across all exercises and populations.

### B06-E8 — 2026 acute fatigue study, abstract only

Refalo et al., [PMID 42458937](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42458937/), DOI `10.1080/02640414.2026.2703381`; **18-person randomized crossover** with back squat and prone row, 65%/85% of 1RM, failure/1-RIR/3-RIR; measurements of *immediate and 24-h fatigue*, not chronic hypertrophy. Closer failure increased several acute neuromuscular, perceptual and metabolic fatigue measures, with differing exercise/load patterns. A crossover acute experiment cannot establish multiweek muscle gains or an individual recovery time clock.

**Disposition:** Keep **acute fatigue cost** and **chronic growth outcome** separate in both research and future explanations.

## Evidence: load/repetition progression versus calendar-forced increases

### B06-P1 — 2022 parallel randomized progression trial, full text read

Plotkin et al., [PMID 36199287](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36199287/), [PMC9528903](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9528903/), DOI `10.7717/peerj.14142`. **43 adults** with prior lower-body training, randomized to increase load (with target reps held steady) or increase repetitions (with load held steady). Eight weeks, quad/calf-focused protocol, twice/week, ultrasound and performance. Most muscle-region changes did not clearly distinguish strategies; rectus femoris point estimate modestly favored repetition progression, and strength point estimate load progression. Limitations: **only lower body**, 8 weeks, variable experience, reported effort varied and some sets terminated before requested failure, ultrasound proxy, self-reported diet.

**Disposition:** Progression can be expressed as improved *repetition performance at comparable load/ROM/effort*, not only heavier dumbbells. No guarantee either method is optimal for every muscle or every training history.

### B06-P2 — 2024 within-limb progression trial, abstract checked

[PMID 38286426](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38286426/), DOI `10.1055/a-2256-5857`. **39 previously untrained adults**, ten weeks of unilateral leg extension, randomized limbs to load versus repetition progression. Both increased vastus lateralis ultrasound CSA and knee-extension strength, with no detected between-limb differences. Full text and within-person correlation/bias treatment not independently qualified.

### B06-P3 — 2026 progressive versus fixed-exposure studies, abstracts checked

- [PMID 41718594](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41718594/), DOI `10.1249/MSS.0000000000003968`: **55 untrained young women** in an 8-week unilateral triceps-extension study; progression by load adjustment when reaching a rep threshold versus **constant load and repetitions**, plus nontraining control. Progression arm showed more local ultrasound thickness gain. Intervention **simultaneously changed the relative challenge over time**; not a test proving load increase per se is uniquely anabolic.
- [PMID 42880344](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42880344/), DOI `10.1055/a-2980-6473`; first published **2026-10-15**: **31 untrained adults**, 12 weeks, unilateral knee-extension progressive versus constant-load training. Both grew; significant relative *change from sessions 18 to 24* favored progression, but **RIR drifted apart** (approximately 0.2 in progression vs 4.1 in fixed-load at final session), as did perceived effort and volume-load. At each individual assessment point, between-condition mCSA differences were not significant. Do not equate a late *change-on-change* effect with a clearly separated endpoint or isolate the causes.

**Reconciliation:** Maintaining a sufficiently challenging stimulus as capacity changes is plausible and supported by these designs, but **repetition progression is a valid alternative to load progression**, and automatically increasing the weight every scheduled week is not an established growth mechanism.

## Bias, overlap and applicability register

| Evidence feature | Observed limitation | What cannot be promoted |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RIR measurements | Targets, self-reported estimates, inferred RIR, failure-by-definition and velocity proxies differ | Exact muscle-specific universal RIR target or automatic `RPE = 10 − RIR` for every scale/set |
| 2025 four-arm study | Withdrawal, group termination and changed lower-body protocol | Numerical injury risk or proof of a single universally superior effort |
| 2026 targeted trials | n=19 for each published report, **same underlying intervention cohort**, corroborated by a third author-group article; unilateral single-joint target/site, eight weeks | Count 38 independent participants, claim external replication, or infer all muscles/schedules equivalent |
| Failure meta-analyses | Overlapping primary samples and mixed operational definitions | Count all review participants as independent replication |
| Progression trials | Cointervention changes in effort, repetitions, volume load and measurement sites | Causal attribution to heavier weight *alone* |
| Fatigue literature | Acute and chronic endpoints inconsistent | Automated deload date or exact rest/recovery hours from soreness, CK, velocity or one bad session |
| Source-books | Authored specialty plans are strength- and user-specific | Transfer their RPE/1RM/testing/deload structure to all Customized users |
| Populations | Mostly healthy young adults; often brief follow-up | Direct numerical extrapolation to novices in recovery, older/clinical populations, or elite trainees |

**No formal trial-by-trial RoB 2, meta-review AMSTAR 2, GRADE grade, supplement-specific adjudication or independent human review has been completed.** Full-text review is targeted, with substantive caveats rather than a certified certainty rating.

## Scientific reconciliation and stop/go

- **Failure is an available technique, not a mandatory universal hypertrophy-maximizer.** Single-set evidence occasionally favors it modestly; other direct trials show similar size change without failure; evidence cannot locate an exact personal optimum.
- **A true effort target is not a true effort observation.** RIR accuracy differs by movement and user familiarization; “unknown” must stay unknown.
- **Fatigue is a cost/constraint**, not a made-up growth penalty or clinical injury predictor; experience, lift mechanics, frequency, session length and tolerance change feasibility.
- **Progression is performance and stimulus maintenance**, not a calendar-triggered plate increase. Reps, load and technique context must remain distinguishable.
- **Deloads/tapers** may serve organization or strength peaking, but the available analysis does not establish exact universal timing, trigger or amount.

**Next:** human review and full-method/supplement extraction for latest controlled studies; independent outcome-specific bias assessments, replicated direct RIR/failure research and validation of feasible progression observables before any population-specific numeric standard. **No generator code or live training data changes.**
