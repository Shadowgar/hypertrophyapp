# Batch 01 — Nippard programming principles versus current evidence

**Research snapshot:** 2026-10-16. **Classification:** source-verified *text-extraction anchors* and an abstract-level literature comparison; not an approved doctrine, systematic review, or individualized training prescription. Literature publication dates below are not the same as the dates through which the reviews searched.

## Executive synthesis

The seven sampled manuals show a coherent practical philosophy: adjust the split to scheduling and skill, track meaningful work, favor quality sets, use effort and progression, conserve recovery, and simplify for constrained schedules. These themes have substantial overlap with modern exercise science. More specific historical claims—particularly one fixed weekly volume range, an independent twice-weekly hypertrophy advantage, routinely maximizing effort, ultra-high-volume "supercompensation," or lengthened-partial superiority—need qualification. Some newer results disagree, but "not consistently superior" does not mean "ineffective".

The strongest candidate for future Customized design is **constraint-first original construction**: preserve user-requested feasible split, fit recoverable work and time, select practical exercises and progression, document uncertainty, and respond conservatively to performance. The weakest candidate is a universal "scientific optimization" score or a single maximal-set/failure/advanced-method formula.

## Claim comparisons

### C01 — Weekly working-set volume: useful, not a universal magic number

**Source observed.** Fundamentals provides a ballpark of roughly 10–15 weekly sets per muscle for many beginners/early intermediates. Upper/Lower discusses roughly 10–20 sets for intermediate/advanced athletes and explicitly notes overlap across compound movements, making raw per-muscle counting difficult (S01, S04).

**Independent evidence.** The 2026 ACSM overview synthesized 137 reviews and associates higher training volume with greater hypertrophy; the 2026 67-study dose-response meta-regression finds a positive association that diminishes as volume increases. The latter's conclusions depend on how indirect sets are attributed; its fractional method is a modeling choice, not a validated universal per-exercise coefficient (P01, P02).

**Preliminary reconciliation.** This broadly supports using dose as an important adjustable variable, **not** prescribing 10, 15 or 20 sets to every user. The sources differ by experience level and historical context. Individual optimal volumes, upper tolerability and muscles' overlapping stimulus remain unresolved.

**Generator candidate — not approved.** Calculate direct/indirect exposure transparently, bound initial dose conservatively, and only offer cautious changes under evidence and recovery constraints. Do not silently stack weak-point sets on top of already heavy programming.

### C02 — Training frequency: distribute quality and adherence before scoring a split

**Source observed.** Fundamentals and Upper/Lower historically assert a benefit of training a muscle more than once weekly even with equated weekly volume, while also stating that additional days help practically distribute work (S01, S04).

**Independent evidence.** The 2026 dose-response analysis finds the independent hypertrophy effect of frequency compatible with a negligible effect, whereas strength shows a more identifiable positive relationship. The 2024 14-study split versus full-body meta-analysis found no statistically significant hypertrophy difference when volume was equated (P02, P03).

**Preliminary reconciliation.** Higher frequency can make volume manageable without guaranteeing superior hypertrophy. Neither nonsignificant difference nor a model estimate establishes all schedules as equivalent for every muscle, experience level or adherence context.

**Generator candidate.** User-selected Full Body, Upper/Lower and PPL should be supported when feasible. No intrinsic "growth score" bonus for split names or an arbitrary hard minimum frequency. Check dose distribution and adjacent-day constraints.

### C03 — Effort and failure: source-specific targets are not global prescriptions

**Source observed.** Fundamentals advises lower initial RPE for beginners and higher effort with growing proficiency (S01, Training Variables pp.85–86). Pure Bodybuilding uses high-effort early/last-set distinctions, frequently harder last sets, and more conservative treatment of movements with high recovery or technique demands (S02, S03).

**Independent evidence.** An exploratory 2024 meta-regression associates training closer to failure with hypertrophy but notes uncertainty in *estimated* repetitions-in-reserve. The 2026 ACSM overview did not find training to momentary failure consistently changed outcomes. A 2025 randomized single-set study of trained adults found generally modest between-condition differences, with some hypertrophy measures favoring failure (P01, P04, P05).

**Preliminary reconciliation.** Meaningful effort matters; universal failure for every working set does not follow. RPE/RIR prediction itself can be noisy, especially in novices. A high-RPE authored block must be preserved exactly in Authored mode, but should **not** become the default for all Customized users.

**Generator candidate.** Effort targets by movement complexity, skill, phase and fatigue, with ability to flag unknown actual RPE. Do not transform prescribed Authored RPE or infer actual effort from target.

### C04 — Rest duration: performance recovery versus fixed countdown

**Source observed.** Both Pure Bodybuilding handbooks emphasize taking enough rest to support subsequent set quality while avoiding needless delay; a precise stopwatch is not obligatory in the source FAQ (S02, S03, FAQ near printed p.45).

**Independent evidence.** A 2024 nine-study Bayesian synthesis suggests a small potential hypertrophy advantage over very short rest intervals, with considerable uncertainty and no clear additional effect beyond about 90 seconds in its pooled comparisons (P06).

**Preliminary reconciliation.** Rest supports subsequent performance, but a one-size-fits-all 60/90/180 second "optimal" timer is not proven. Protocols, exercises, load, fatigue and goal vary.

**Generator candidate.** Practical rest ranges with user control, separate target and *measured actual* rest, and disclose uncertainty. Do not auto-prune prescribed Authored rest.

### C05 — Lengthened partials: a useful option but no blanket superiority rule

**Source observed.** Pure Bodybuilding highlights lengthened partials; Phase 2 uses them both as exercise variations and sometimes as beyond-failure extensions. These are distinct training operations (S02, S03, Intensity Techniques near printed pp.12–14).

**Independent evidence.** A multisite 2025 RCT (n=297 completing pre-randomization assignment, with actual outcome retention requiring full-text check) found small group-level contrasts between lengthened partials and full ROM in the tested arm/thigh outcomes; the pre-specified strict equivalence threshold was **not conclusively met**. A smaller 2026 trained-men study found no detectable difference for vastus lateralis over 8 weeks. A 2026 systematic review suggests longer versus shorter muscle-length training may have benefits but notes mixed, methodologically constrained evidence; longer-muscle-length exposure is not identical to lengthened-only partials (P07, P08, P09).

**Preliminary reconciliation.** Do not collapse "lengthened training", "lengthened-only partial repetitions" and "partials after failure" into one scientific claim. Effects may differ by muscle, movement and full vs partial loading.

**Generator candidate.** Default to competent controllable ROM and allow separately qualified exercise-specific partial techniques as optional variations, not mandatory or universally superior.

### C06 — Periodization and deloads: planning tools, not proof of extraordinary growth

**Source observed.** Upper/Lower organizes fatigue through effort waves; Phase 2 Pure Bodybuilding uses an easier introductory period and a week-6 semi-deload; Ultimate PPL presents distinct Base, Maximum Overload and high-volume Supercompensation phases (S03–S05).

**Independent evidence.** A 2022 35-study volume-equated meta-analysis favored periodization for 1RM strength but did not find a significant general hypertrophy difference. A 2026 network meta-analysis found no advanced resistance training method consistently superior for hypertrophy in the populations studied (P10, P11).

**Preliminary reconciliation.** Structured training blocks can aid manageability and strength goals, but no evidence reviewed yet verifies a universal hypertrophic "supercompensation" gain from a sudden ultra-high-volume phase. Different authored models may work without their precise waves being causally optimal.

**Generator candidate.** Stable exercise anchors, manageable planned changes, optional recovery-phase adjustments where justified, and avoid promising special growth from a phase label. Retain authored source weeks without reinterpretation.

### C07 — Time efficiency: valid objective, explicit trade-offs

**Source observed.** Essentials is designed around roughly 45-minute sessions, 2–5 times weekly, with reduced per-exercise set counts and greater emphasis on logging effortful performance (S06, About This Program printed pp.5–6).

**Independent evidence.** A 2025 narrative review found supersets can reduce session time while often retaining broadly similar long-term adaptations, though the included techniques and protocols are heterogeneous. A 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis of 12 drop-set studies (n=274) did not identify a statistically significant hypertrophy difference against traditional sets but did find substantially greater perceived exertion (P12, P13).

**Preliminary reconciliation.** A shorter plan may be very useful and still productive; it need not claim identical outcome at every total dose. Advanced methods are tools to fit a time budget, not general efficacy multipliers.

**Generator candidate.** Independent time-constrained mode with explicit upper bound on session duration, accounting for actual work, rest, setup and warm-ups; antagonist pairing only when reasonable; never call reduced dose "identical" to a longer plan without evidence.

### C08 — Progressive overload: logbook-driven adaptation, not the same as forced weekly load increases

**Source observed.** Fundamentals defines progressive overload in terms of increasingly demanding training; Essentials emphasizes comparable weight/repetition performance rather than simply adding weekly sets (S01, S06).

**Independent evidence.** A newly published October 15, 2026, unilateral knee-extension trial in 31 previously untrained adults found greater late-phase hypertrophy change with a progressive-load condition than fixed load, but the conditions also differed in relative effort (RIR), perceived exertion and cumulative volume load (P14). This does **not** isolate load progression as the sole cause of the result.

**Preliminary reconciliation.** Track progression and preserve sufficiently challenging effort; don't force load increases despite poor performance or falsely claim that increasing weight week by week independently causes more growth.

**Generator candidate.** Reuse existing completed-comparable-exposure decisions (increase/hold/decrease/monitor), let the user override, and abstain where load/variant/effort comparability is missing.

### C09 — Returning after a training break: distinct bridge concept, unproven fixed recipe

**Source observed.** Comeback Bridge distinguishes a complete layoff, partial training interruption and continued heavy training; it offers specific short return-to-training paths (S07, Program Explained pp.6–7).

**Independent evidence.** In a 2024 randomized comparison of periodic versus continuous training, participants with a temporary break regained lost size and strength comparatively rapidly on retraining, though the study does not validate an arbitrary four-week re-entry prescription. A small mechanistic human study found retained myonuclei after detraining but could not establish a clear superior re-training hypertrophy response (P15, P16).

**Preliminary reconciliation.** Re-entry deserves a separate, conservative status and individual adjustment, not an assumption that everyone retains identical load capability or will regain within a fixed number of weeks.

**Generator candidate.** A transparent re-entry profile based on recent training, observed performance and tolerance; progressive progression after real exposures, not presuming performance from old PRs.

### C10 — Exercise selection, substitutions and specialization need an actual ontology

**Source observed.** Pure Bodybuilding discusses substitution decisions in terms of target, resistance/length bias and exercise effectiveness; Phase 2 narrows additions for arms because existing arm work is already high. Program sheets contain explicit alternative options (S02, S03, W01–W02).

**Independent evidence.** The literature checked for Batch 01 does **not** establish equivalence of each source-listed substitute, a universal exercise ranking, or a validated per-muscle specialization multiplier. These are therefore **not yet evidence-backed** independent generator rules.

**Generator candidate.** Build exercise metadata with movement, primary/secondary action, resistance profile, equipment, load basis, fatigue/skill needs and source of validation. Separate biomechanics inference from demonstrated human hypertrophy outcome. Use explicit constraint-satisfying substitution rules, no magic exercise-score ranking.

### C11 — RPE, date and source parser reliability is a scientific problem too

**Source observed.** The source programs have per-set/slot effort and phase differences. The connector's text conversion for at least one Excel sheet shows some numeric-looking values as dates. The original workbook cells were **not** inspected in this batch.

**Evidence implication.** A source transcription error can corrupt the entire research conclusion and cannot be fixed by adding scientific papers. Exact source numbers in source-authored programs require raw workbook files, cell types, formats, formulas, sheet references and provenance checks.

**Generator candidate.** Do not use text-projected spreadsheet numeric values as approved rules. Preserve authored prescriptions as their own fidelity-qualified source, separate from summarized scientific doctrine.
