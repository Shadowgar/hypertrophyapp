# M4-R Batch 05 — Volume, frequency and short-session evidence appraisal

**Researched:** 2026-10-16. **Status:** targeted, original scientific appraisal; **not** a systematic review, formal AMSTAR 2/RoB 2/GRADE rating, approved numerical prescription, or generator implementation. This continues Batches 01–04 without repeating the completed 57-PDF/21-XLSX audits.

## Questions and bounded source batch

1. What does human evidence distinguish about *productive low volume* versus *maximal expected hypertrophy*?
2. Can the app quantify indirect compound-exercise work as interchangeable fractional hypertrophy sets?
3. Does evidence support choosing a split by label, or choosing a feasible distribution of work?
4. Can “45-minute” literature/source programs establish an unconditional session-time guarantee?

**New private-source reading:** two previously unappraised editions, *The Essentials Program 2x* (archive PDF hash begins `5330cdb3`, 66 physical pages) and *The Essentials Program 3x* (hash begins `2aafd830`, 78 pages). Only author explanations/FAQ/periodization sections were screened through Drive's bounded text-extraction view; no exercise tables or proprietary prescription sequences copied. The pre-existing [PDF audit](pdf-audit-57.csv) supplies identity/page counts, **not** a new visual page-by-page check of the claims. Both variants share explanatory material, so repeated assertions are **one source family, not two independent studies**.

- **2x:** introductory explanation and FAQ (printed pp.5–9) positions two days and hard, economical working sets as a practical solution to limited available time, with logbook-driven progression. This is a commercial program design, not a comparison proving the highest possible hypertrophy from two sessions.
- **3x:** introduction/FAQ (printed pp.5–9), split/periodization and progression (printed pp.32–35) uses a mixed Full Body/Upper/Lower week. It emphasizes exercise consistency across blocks, tracked reps/loads and optional noncompeting supersets. The original split is *source-owned*, not a template for Customized.
- **Both:** marketing-level roughly 45-minute target is qualified by a FAQ admission that longer warmups/rest may extend sessions to **roughly 45–60 minutes**. The manual's time example is not a measured cross-user guarantee. Source substitution options are author choices, not demonstrated equivalent growth outcomes.
- **Both:** the manuals cite low-dose training studies to defend the ability to *make gains*, which must not be rewritten as evidence that low dose *maximizes* gains. Anecdotal bodybuilding examples and unverified individual muscle-gain-per-month estimates are not eligible as generator calibration data.

## Selected published research — paper-by-paper

### E05-A — Randomized multi-volume intervention, **full text inspected**

Schoenfeld et al., 2019, *Resistance Training Volume Enhances Muscle Hypertrophy but Not Strength in Trained Men*, [PMID 30153194](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30153194/), [rights-eligible PMC6303131](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6303131/), DOI `10.1249/MSS.0000000000001764`.

- **Population and assignment:** 45 resistance-trained men (average reported age ~24, training experience ~4.4 years) randomized to 1, 3 or 5 working sets **per exercise/session**. **34 analyzed** (11/12/11); 11 dropouts included four for personal reasons, three noncompliance, two training-related injury and two unrelated injury. Trial acknowledged dropping below its a priori 36-participant power target.
- **Dose/comparator:** same seven-exercise Full Body routine, three supervised nonconsecutive days/week for eight weeks; 8–12 reps to momentary concentric failure, ~90-second between-set rest. Reported mean *session training time* about **13, 40 and 68 minutes** for 1/3/5 sets. Thus **frequency was held constant** while sets, time and likely fatigue increased. Do not infer a superior frequency or the effect of varied RIR from this comparison.
- **Muscle outcomes:** ultrasound thickness at single anatomical sites; greater change in five- versus one-set condition for elbow flexors, mid-thigh/rectus femoris and lateral thigh outcomes; elbow extensor group comparison not statistically significant. Bench/squat 1RM changes were not detectably different among these conditions. An observed null comparison is **not** proven equivalence.
- **Important limitations:** considerable dropout; some further unusable ultrasound measurements; short duration, younger male-trained sample, unblinded intervention and uncertain assessor blinding; selected mid-belly thickness instead of whole-muscle MRI; upper-body work exclusively multijoint and ambiguous attribution of secondary muscle stimulus; all sets trained to failure, not representative of mixed-effort routines; source paper admits possible novelty effects of reducing volume and switching to failure.
- **Appraisal disposition:** convincing within-this-protocol evidence against equating “some growth at low dose” with “no additional growth from higher dose.” Not a validated universal dose ceiling/minimum, safe load prescription, or fixed 0.5 indirect-set multiplier. RoB 2 domain rating **not assigned**; allocation concealment, missing outcome handling and selective-reporting plan need independent verification.

### E05-B — 2023 Bayesian network review, **full text inspected**

Currier et al., 2023, *Resistance training prescription for muscle strength and hypertrophy in healthy adults*, [PMID 37414459](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37414459/), [PMC10579494](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10579494/), DOI `10.1136/bjsports-2023-106807`.

- Registered synthesis, duplicate screening/extraction and RoB 2 assessments, searches ending **February 7, 2022**. Hypertrophy network: **119 studies, 3,364 participants, 11 prescription nodes**, 24 directly compared node-pairs. The nodes *coarsely categorize* load, single/multiple sets and 1/2/3+ session frequency; they do not encode every muscle, indirect exercise, equipment or actual set dose.
- In the authors' full-text RoB overview for hypertrophy, **18% high risk, 82% some concerns/moderate and 0% low risk**. They report imprecision in many *between-active-prescription* comparisons and explicitly reject interpreting statistically nonsignificant contrasts as proof of equivalence. Multiple-set options rank highly for hypertrophy, not a warrant for a single exact prescription.
- Evidence comes largely from supervised, healthy adults. Some network nodes were extremely sparse and the framework excluded periodized or non-matching plans, unsupervised training, athletes/military and many chronic-condition populations. Transitivity, varying measurement sites and imperfect comparator matching constrain personalized inference.
- **Appraisal disposition:** supports a broad distinction between doing resistance training versus none and the practical value of multi-set programming, while **not** producing an “optimal” split or guaranteed hypertrophy score. This review overlaps primary trials with subsequent reviews; no additive participant-count claims. Independent AMSTAR 2 assessment outstanding.

### E05-C — 2024 split-versus-Full-Body meta-analysis, **publisher full text/methods inspected**

Ramos-Campo et al., 2024, [PMID 38595233](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38595233/), DOI `10.1519/JSC.0000000000004774` ([journal article](https://journals.lww.com/nsca-jscr/fulltext/2024/07000/efficacy_of_split_versus_full_body_resistance.19.aspx)).

- Fourteen studies, **392 participants**, split routines compared against Full Body, with volume-equated analyses. Search through **June 23, 2023**; eligibility constrained to **adults 18–39**, with only two women-only samples and one mixed-sex sample among 14 studies. Reported mean age approximately 25 years. The review was PROSPERO registered and its authors classified **10/14 included RCTs low overall risk of bias, four some concerns**.
- No statistically significant pooled differences in assessed elbow-flexor/extensor or vastus lateralis size or lean-body-mass proxies; several outcome comparisons use only **four to six trials**, with wide intervals. The authors' review evidence is not a head-to-head test of *every* Upper/Lower, PPL, hybrid, twice-per-week or rolling calendar arrangement.
- **Appraisal disposition:** do not award a split-label hypertrophy advantage. Neither nonsignificance nor group averages prove full physiological equivalence in advanced/older/time-poor adults; screen direct outcome measures separately from lean-mass proxies. Independent review-quality scoring remains pending.

### E05-D — 2026 direct/indirect dose–frequency meta-regression, **abstract and disclosures verified, full text not qualified**

Pelland et al., issue dated February 2026 (online December 4, 2025), [PMID 41343037](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/), DOI `10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w`.

- **67 studies, 2,058 people**, approximately **79% male** and mean age ~25 years. The authors compared coding of an indirect set as **0, 0.5 or 1** equivalent units and reported better relative *model evidence* for the fractional coding. Volume showed diminishing predicted returns; conditional frequency model gave less decisive evidence for a nonzero independent hypertrophy association than for strength.
- **Critical interpretation:** a model preference for “0.5” does **not** validate that any one row, elbow flexor, squat, press or equipment variant biologically produces precisely half the hypertrophy of a directly targeted set. Outcome sites and exercise specificity, participant features, loads, proximity to failure and base training all vary. A meta-regression across different protocols does not replace an individual-level randomized dose calibration.
- Authors disclosed professional fitness coaching/content roles, and reported supplementary data and analysis code on OSF. The journal's **published complete full text/supplement and primary-study coding table have not been independently appraised in this batch**. No formal AMSTAR 2 rating, curve-based minimum, personal optimum or automatic direct/indirect coefficient is approved.

## Reconciliation across the four studies

- **Not independent totals.** A primary volume RCT may also be contained within one or more of the meta-analyses; the 2023 network, 2024 split review and 2026 dose-regression may overlap. Never sum their studies/participants.
- **Strength is not hypertrophy.** Low time/dose can produce useful strength change, and higher dose can produce additional site-specific muscle growth; neither outcome is a deterministic proxy for the other.
- **Distribution is not dosage.** Frequency, split name, number of hard sets, indirect compound involvement, effort, trained muscle length, and session duration need distinct fields.
- **Population matters.** Young healthy volunteers dominate the directly examined comparisons. No high-certainty numerical extrapolation to older trainees, re-entry, pain/medical restrictions, highly trained lifters or women from these pooled means.
- **Time is a competing objective.** The measured 13/40/68-minute training durations are *from one tightly supervised trial*, not a reliable timing oracle for public gyms, home equipment, commute, setup, warmups, supersets or wait times.
